Teacher-creator philosophy

PEDAGOGY BEFORE TECHNOLOGY

Designing learning experiences worth building.

AI has made it easier than ever for teachers to create apps, games, simulations and interactive learning environments. But just because we can build something doesn’t mean we should.

My starting point is always the learning.

1. Start with a real learning challenge

What do my students struggle with? What opportunities are missing from the learning experience? What would help them understand, practise or apply their knowledge?

A good learning tool begins with a meaningful educational need, not an exciting new feature.

2. Design the learning before the technology

Before building anything, I consider:

  • The learners: Who are they, and what support do they need?
  • The learning outcomes: What should they know or be able to do?
  • The experience: What should students actively think, explore, create or practise?
  • The evidence: How will we know that learning has taken place?

This reflects an important principle in educational research: technology is most useful when it supports clearly defined teaching and learning goals (Education Endowment Foundation, 2019).

3. Make technology serve pedagogy

Technology can create opportunities that are difficult to offer through traditional materials alone.

Students can practise professional conversations in a virtual restaurant, explore the causes of social movements through simulations, or reflect on their own AI fluency through interactive assessments.

But the technology is not the learning. The thinking, decisions, practice and reflection it makes possible are what matter.

4. Keep the human at the centre

Good learning design considers accessibility, different ways of engaging with content, meaningful feedback and opportunities for independent thinking.

AI can support practice, provide scaffolding and open up new possibilities. It should not take over the intellectual work students need to do themselves or replace the teacher’s professional judgement.

5. Build, test and learn

I see every creation as a prototype. I test it, observe how learners interact with it and ask whether it actually supports the intended learning.

Sometimes the result is an app. Sometimes it is a game, a simulation or simply a better-designed learning activity. And sometimes, no new technology is needed.

My teacher-creator principle
Pedagogy before technology. Purpose before features. Learning before novelty.

Teachers do not need to become software developers. But with the possibilities offered by AI, they can become more active creators of the learning experiences they know their students need.

Technology should follow the learning, not lead it.

Research informing my approach: Education Endowment Foundation: Using Digital Technology to Improve Learning · CAST: Universal Design for Learning Guidelines

Creating with care

THE VIBE-CODE OF ETHICS

Creating with AI is powerful. Creating without thinking can put students at risk.

Vibe coding gives teachers extraordinary new possibilities. A teacher with an idea can turn it into a working app, game or simulation in an afternoon. I believe in this, and it is why I build.

But there is a risk that is easy to miss. Most teachers who build with AI are not careless. The problem is that the line between a classroom resource and an information system is almost invisible to the person crossing it.

The invisible line

A quiz is a resource. A quiz that saves names and scores is a system that holds information about children. It happens in one sentence to an AI: “Save the student’s score,” “Let students log in,” “Send the results to a spreadsheet.” The app still works perfectly. But working and safe are different tests.

A polished app proves nothing

AI can produce professional-looking software in minutes. A beautiful interface tells you nothing about where the data goes, who can see it or who is responsible for it. In education we share resources freely, which is wonderful, but an app is not a worksheet. We need to start asking of software what we already ask of sources: who made this, and can I trust what is behind it?

Pedagogy is at risk too

The same applies to students creating with generative AI. Giving a class an AI tool with no scaffolding, no guidance on ethical use and no structure produces fast output, not learning. Without a plan, it can also mean students uploading their faces, voices or work to services nobody has checked.

How I build

My tools do not ask students to sign up. They do not ask for personal information, and they do not collect or store any data. There is nothing to leak, because nothing is kept.

I choose this on purpose, and I think it is the safest starting point for any teacher.

Five questions before the first prompt

  1. What learning problem am I solving, and for whom?
  2. Does this need any personal data? If not, design it so it never does.
  3. If data is stored, where does it go and who can see it?
  4. Is any student text, image or voice being sent to an AI service?
  5. If something goes wrong, who is responsible, and does my school know?

A simple rule

If your tool needs sign-ups, saved results, uploads or connections to other services, pause. That is the moment to involve your school’s data protection and safeguarding leads. Test and demo with made-up data, never real students’ information.

Explore the Vibe-Code of Ethics course

I have created The Vibe-Code of Ethics, a course designed to help educators become more responsible and reflective creators of AI-powered digital tools. It explores how to combine the creative possibilities of vibe coding with pedagogical thinking, ethical awareness, data protection, accessibility, security and human oversight. The course encourages teachers to look beyond what they can build with AI and consider whether they should build it, who it serves and what responsibilities come with creating technology for learners. It is an invitation to learn, reflect and rethink how we create with AI in education.

Keep building, with your eyes open

I am not asking teachers to build less. I am asking them to build thoughtfully, and to talk openly about what we create. Technology should follow the learning, and ethics should come before the first line of code.

About

Barbara Anna Zielonka

High school teacher in Norway, founder of AI Advisory Boards, author and lifelong learner.

Educator Empowerment Award 2024Teacher Innovation finalist 2026Author, June 2025AI in Education, HVL

Exploring AI from day one

As a high school teacher in Norway with a lifelong learning mindset, I started exploring AI the moment the shift began. That same month I founded the global blog AI Advisory Boards, where I have published over 1,600 posts on lesson plans, tool tests, AI apps, games and pedagogical reflections, for a daily community of 350 to 500 educators worldwide.

This grassroots commitment to teacher empowerment was recognised when I won the Educator Empowerment Award at the AI for Education Awards in 2024, and later became a finalist for the Teacher Innovation Award at the 2026 awards.

Sharing and teaching

I share my expertise, reflections and practical ideas about AI in education on LinkedIn and X, contributing to international conversations about how AI can transform teaching and learning. I also run workshops for fellow educators, helping teachers discover practical applications and recognise their own capacity to become creators and innovators rather than simply consumers of educational technology.

Rooted in student collaboration

My work is built with students. In 2023 and 2024 I established student-led AI advisory boards at my school, creating open frameworks that other schools can replicate. I am currently pursuing formal studies in AI in Education at the Western Norway University of Applied Sciences (HVL).

Learning to build through vibecoding

Over the past 15 months I have taught myself to build interactive apps, simulations and games through vibecoding: describing what I want to create, working with AI to build it, and refining it through testing. I am a fluent user of Google AI Studio, Lovable, GitHub, Cloudflare and Netlify, which lets me take an idea from a classroom challenge to a live, shareable tool. In that time I have built more than 200 apps, simulations and games, and this site showcases a selection of them. Every creation on this site was made this way, and it is the clearest proof of my belief that teachers can be creators of technology, not just consumers.

The book

In June 2025 I published Embracing AI in Education: My Journey and Lessons for Educators. It shares real-world experience of integrating AI into teaching, explores how AI can enhance student engagement and agency, and highlights uniquely human skills: critical thinking, creativity, emotional intelligence, resilience, adaptability and ethical judgement. I argue for rethinking the educator’s role so that technology strengthens, rather than replaces, the human dimension of education.
